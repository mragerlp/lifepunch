## 1. The law

**1. ONE DRIVE AT A TIME.** Editor DRIVE is exclusive and board-named: at any
moment exactly one seat holds DRIVE, declared on the BOARD by Bloodwave's grant.
All other seats are OBSERVE-only.

**2. DRIVE = TREE HANDS.** The editor writes to the checkout (scene saves,
prefab bakes, hotload artifacts). A DRIVE grant is therefore a full seat swap
under the relief clause: the DRIVE holder is THE implementer seat for the
duration; the other implementer (Opus/Red) does not touch the tree until
swap-back. Swaps occur at arc/slice boundaries, never mid-slice. The "Codex
proposal-only on the canonical tree" clause is **SUSPENDED** while Codex holds
DRIVE and **resumes on swap-back**.

**3. GOVERNOR CLAUSE CARRIES.** Codex keeps DRIVE while it keeps flagging its
own unverified edges. If it asserts where it should flag, or slips on hard work,
Bloodwave routes DRIVE back to Opus. Opus is ALWAYS-ON STANDBY: blocker
gap-closer, hard-case escalation, and proof/merge runs at Bloodwave's discretion.

> **BLOODWAVE'S INTENT, VERBATIM SPIRIT:** Codex has earned a full chance — it
> works the same corners at lower cost. Opus's standby explicitly includes the
> REVIEW-AND-ENHANCE lane: second eyes on Codex's implemented work, quality lift
> where Codex's eyes fall short of 20/20, and the lengthier detailed passes where
> Opus's process earns its cost. Both seats — and every agent on this large,
> quirky game codebase — are still LEARNING it, plugins included; individual
> misses are learning data and grading input, not grounds for demotion. Demotion
> is reserved for a sustained quality pattern, ruled by Bloodwave, not inferred
> by a seat.

**4. UNCHANGED:** two-key actions; ULTRACODE (terminal/Red console invocation
only); observed/chair phase discipline binds whoever drives; Sensor Law; commit
discipline (proof gates, attribution-clean) binds the DRIVE holder identically.

## 2. The circle, reshaped (Bloodwave, 2026-07-13)

Opus and Codex have **THE SAME ROLE** — they always did: implement, study
through their implementation, consult. They are two equally capable models with
different physics: Opus = slower, deeper, rich context/explanations; SOL 5.6 =
same capability spent as speed, less narration, more throughput. **Neither is
senior.**

The loop is no longer a fixed 3-way circle (Me/Fable → Opus → Cornerman → back);
it is **Me/Fable → Opus AND/OR Codex by task fit → back.** The implementers prep
and enhance EACH OTHER — cross-review strengthens both seats' perception of the
codebase; each pass through the other's eyes is training data for the pair.

## 3. Cornerman's place

Green either scans a larger packet (bulk audit, daily model) or stands
**REVIEW-READY** with the Qwen Coder model loaded — a third, independent set of
code eyes for back-checks when a task needs a hand past Opus/Codex. Used
correctly, the coder model raises both productivity and quality: it is an
**enhancer of the implementers' understanding**, not just an auditor.

**Model swap on CORNERMAN is a Bloodwave console act.**

See `CORNERMAN_CONSULT_DOCTRINE.md` for the consult lane this opens.

## 4. Parallelism clause

Opus/Codex/Cornerman may run two tasks simultaneously **ONLY when the surfaces
are disjoint and cannot interfere** (e.g., one seat driving the editor while
another reviews exported diffs while Green scans read-only). The invariants are
untouched: one DRIVE at a time, one pair of tree hands at a time, board-named.

**Overlap is a Bloodwave call per pairing, not a seat inference.**
