## THE THREE-LAYER SHAPE

Every operation in LIFEPUNCH is built from the same three layers. A job's
risk profile is simply **how much of it is nailed to the floor.**

```
TABLET            job equipment · issued with the job · persists through death
   |  controls, remotely operates
HUB + TERMINAL    spawnable · ownable · upgradeable · STEALABLE, HACKABLE
   |  links to
PERMANENT ENTITY  fixed on the map · the vault, the pile, the data center, the drop
```

**Tablets are safe. Installations are exposed.** This is the whole security
economy in two sentences.

- **Miner** — layer 2 only. All installation, no mobility. His rig does not
  travel; that is precisely why his base is worth raiding.
- **Chemist** — Drug Tablet; labs fixed, selling mobile.
- **Hacker** — Hacker Tablet; almost nothing fixed. The predator owns nothing.
- **Banker** — Bank Tablet (remote money movement, machine control) + a
  spawnable hub and terminal + **two** permanent installations.
- **FBI / Mayor** — Police Tablet + spawnable hub and terminal + the
  government's two permanent installations.

**Rule:** anyone whose operation must move gets a tablet. Anyone whose
operation is a fixed installation gets a terminal. Most serious jobs have both.

Tablets are **job-issued equipment**: they spawn with the job, survive death,
and are never looted. They are identity, not inventory.

## THE TWO ELECTED OFFICES

**The Mayor and the Banker are both chosen by the people whose money is at
stake, and their interests are opposed.** The Mayor extracts; the Banker
shelters capital from extraction. Two poles. A political system, not a job list.

- **Banker** — vote-required, with **Bank Guard** slots attached. A job that
  holds other people's money and can be raided into ruin cannot be a slot
  claimed by whoever clicked first at 3 AM.
- **Mayor** — sets the tax rate (0-30%), spends City Funds, holds the
  government's keys.

**Bank Guards are the depositors' answer to a public-only FBI.** The state
protects the state's money; depositors hire their own defense.

### The Bank building is a shared workspace

Like the PD and the Hospital, the **Bank building is a class commons** — the
Banker and the Bank Guards work it by rule; no basing, no locking out the
public lobby. But the **FUND remains the Banker's charge**: vote-required
owner, personal entities as the deductible, reputation at stake. **Obligation
binds the space; accountability binds the person** — the same split as the
Hospital, one layer up in what's at risk.

### The Bank Guard charter — allegiance monopoly, economic freedom

The Guard's **allegiance** is monopolized, not his economy. He may **not crew
with raiders, base with gangs, or take contracts outside the Bank** — he lives
in the Bank. But he is a **full economic citizen**: he may run his own printers
and mining rigs inside it, invest in the fund like any depositor, and receive
whatever wage the Banker chooses to pay.

**Guard pay is voluntary, which makes it a reputation surface.** A stingy
Banker gets guards who quit; a generous one gets a standing army. And a Guard
who invests his wage back into the fund he defends is **guarding his own
money** — loyalty made literal.

Contrast with the Medic's freedom: jobs holding other people's money don't get
other *people* — but they still get their **own**. The Medic's associations are
his because he holds no capital; the Guard's allegiance is bound because he
holds everyone's, yet his purse stays his.

### Incorruptibility — the one absolute

**Bankers and Bank Guards may NEVER assist a Bank Raid, in any way.** This is
the **only incorruptibility rule in the canon.** Everything else is corruptible
by design — the Mayor may be a villain, the FBI may be starved, the Dealer may
be bribed. The Bank alone is protected from its own operators, **because
everything else in the economy depends on it being boring.** A bank you cannot
trust is not a bank; the whole stock-market fiction collapses the moment its
keepers can be turned. **This is why the allegiance monopoly exists** — the
Guard's loyalty is walled off precisely so it can never be sold.

## THE FUND, NOT THE BANK

**The Banker's vaults are not a bank. They are the server's only stock market.**

A player's DXRP portal **bank balance is always safe** and exists whether or
not anyone plays Banker. Money moved into the Banker's vaults is **voluntarily
at-risk capital**, seeking yield. Rob the fund and investors lose their
*investment* — never their bank balance.

Yield is **backed by real production** (the Master Miner and fees), never
minted on a schedule (`ECONOMY_DOCTRINE`, Banker charter). **Players are
buying exposure to BTC production without running a rig.** The Banker is the
index fund of the mining economy.

### The two lanes

| | **Cash pool** (the pile) | **BTC vault** |
|---|---|---|
| Deposit | cash, in person or via the Banker's ATM | BTC, wired remotely from any hub |
| Tax | **yes** — taxes eat the yield | **none** |
| Threat | armed robbery — a crew kicks the door | hacking — one competent man with the right terminal |
| Loss profile | percentage haircut, long cooldown | catastrophic; the vault can be emptied |

**Untaxed is not safest.** The BTC lane's price is *catastrophic tail risk*.
Robbery is a haircut; a vault breach is a disaster.

### The pile is visible

The money-pile entity **grows with deposits and visibly shrinks after a raid.**
Everyone who walks past the bank knows how good a day it has been, and knows
when it has been robbed. **Panic is information, and information is roleplay.**

A fat pile advertises trust — which attracts both investors and robbers.
**Success is the vulnerability.**

### The Banker bleeds first

**The Banker's personal entities are the deductible.** A successful raid takes
his weapon shipments, his money printers, his personal mining rig — *before*
depositors take their percentage haircut.

**He cannot get rich while his investors are robbed.** Skin in the game by
construction, not by rule. This is the sentence that makes the job trustworthy.

### Robbery mechanics

- **Cash pile:** open to any armed crew. Percentage-based variable payout
  (the drug-drop math), on the **PD-raid cooldown**. The desperate crime.
- **Crypto vault:** targetable **only** by a Hacker holding an **Advanced
  Hacker Terminal**, whose unlock is sold exclusively by the **Black Market
  Dealer**. The sophisticated crime.

Total loss of the pile requires repeated successful raids across a long
cooldown — a server-wide event, not a Tuesday night. **The cooldown is what
makes withdrawal a viable defensive act:** there is a window, everyone knows
it, and the smart money moves. Players will invent the phrase *bank run*
without being taught it.

Reputation is persistent, unmodeled, and entirely emergent. Depositors will
ask a Banker his security tier before trusting him. A robbed Banker who
under-invested will not be trusted twice.

## THE GOVERNMENT: THE SAME MACHINE, OPPOSITE POLITICS

The Mayor and the Banker run **structurally identical institutions**. One is
private and taxed; one is public and funded *by* that tax. One codebase, two
institutions.

| | **Banker** | **Government** |
|---|---|---|
| Cash installation | the pile (investor capital) | **City Funds** (the treasury; exists in DXRP today) |
| BTC installation | the Crypto Vault + Master Miner | **the Data Center** (mines, never pays a person) |
| Beneficiary | investors, pro-rata | the public |
| Defense | Bank Guards (hired) | FBI (public mandate) |

### The Data Center

Mines BTC exactly as a player rig does (`PayoutTarget = CityFunds`). Its BTC
is **converted to City Funds cash hourly, at the current Mayor's tax rate.**
No Mayor in office -> the default rate holds. **It is never manually
withdrawn.**

Consequences, all intended:

- **The Mayor cannot loot the treasury.** He sets a rate; the conversion is
  automatic; the destination is public. The classic corrupt-mayor failure
  mode dies without a single anti-abuse rule.
- **A 0% Mayor starves the city** — no conversion, no bounties, no lotteries,
  no FBI premiums, and hackers roam. Everyone loves him and the city rots.
- **A 30% Mayor floods the treasury** and drives all private capital into the
  untaxable BTC lane, shrinking the very tax base he is squeezing.
- Both extremes are self-punishing. **The equilibrium moves. A Mayor's
  competence is legible in the state of the city.** That is a real election
  issue.

### The FBI is politically dependent

FBI premiums for preventing attacks on the government's funds are **paid from
City Funds**, which the Mayor's rate controls. **A Mayor can starve the FBI,
and the city feels it when he does.** The rest is, per doctrine, *charity work
or their duty*.

Guard against total collapse: City Funds also receives ordinary taxation, so
the Data Center is the treasury's **growth engine, not its only engine**. A
hacker crew that takes the Data Center down cripples the FBI's premiums
without instantly abolishing the police. The vulnerability is real; it is not
total.

### The Hierarchy of Protection

Law-enforcement response has a **fixed priority order** — the first priority
ordering in the canon. **State first, capital second, citizens last:**

```
1. PD Raid    → protect the Mayor    (the state)
2. Bank Raid  → protect the Vault    (capital)
3. Hospital   → protect the People   (citizens)
```

The consequences are intended, not accidents to be patched:

- **Every tier can be pulled off by attacking the tier above it.** Hit the PD
  and nobody comes anywhere — the Bank and the Hospital both go undefended
  while the state saves itself. **Diversion is a legitimate tactic**, and it is
  *exactly why depositors fund Bank Guards*: state protection is conditional,
  so capital buys its own.
- **The Hospital sitting last is a standing political grievance.** Medics will
  say so, loudly, and they will be right. That the people come last is a
  designed injustice the server is meant to argue about — a live election
  issue, not a bug.

## THE MAYOR'S TAX IS A BEHAVIOURAL LEVER

**BTC investment with the Banker is untaxed. Cash investment is not.**

Therefore the tax rate does not merely set how much the state takes — it
decides **where the server's capital lives.**

```
high tax -> cash deposits dry up -> capital converts to BTC -> BTC demand spikes
         -> miners prosper -> the Black Market's conversion window gets busy
         -> the taxable base shrinks -> the treasury the Mayor squeezed for
            shrinks with it
```

The Mayor's own policy sets the price of the currency he cannot tax, and he
learns about it — with a delay — by watching the bank's deposit mix. **The
Banker sees both lanes and knows which way the wind blows before anyone
else.** That is what "financial fluency" means as a skill a player can
actually exercise: he can read the ratio.

The Banker's premium is honest: he is paid to be the bridge, and the bridge
sits exactly on the tax boundary.

## THE HOSPITAL — THE THIRD INSTITUTION

*(filed 2026-07-09; STATUS: all PLANNED — no build, no numbers. The medic
class and armor assets exist; systems arrive through their lane in the
sequencing map.)*

**The three-layer shape, third instance:**

```
PATIENT TABLET    job-issued, mobile — response work moves
   |  controls, remotely operates
MED STATIONS      installation-class · upgradeable (tiers per Law B = BTC;
   |              the consumables they dispense = cash)
THE HOSPITAL      permanent map entity
```

**RULE: the Hospital is SHARED, like the PD** — a class commons, no elected
owner. Medics work there by rule.

The commons rule binds the **building, not the Medic.** A Medic may freelance
with any raiders, base with anyone, and run his own affiliations freely — the
one thing he may not do is **close the Hospital off from other Medics.** The
building is open to every Medic by rule; no Medic may lock it, wall it, or
claim exclusive use. Shared access is the invariant; the Medic's associations
are his own.

**Commons-ownership ruling:** shared station tiers are **per-station, funded
by whoever pays, owned by no one** — public goods, privately funded. The
free-rider tension is intended roleplay. Role-vacancy: stations persist,
heal nobody without a Medic.

**Armor:** the med station gains armor-dispensing tiers — the **STATION is
the entity** (BTC tiers); armor remains cash-class equipment it produces.
Law B intact; armor itself never carries persistent tiers.

**The Stim War:** the Black Market Dealer sells stimulants — cheap,
effective, carrying health risks and status effects that **only Medic items
cure**. The black market creates the ailment; the Hospital sells the cure.
The Medic class gains a villain and a persuasion role; the Dealer gains a
second chemical economy (the Chemist's drugs are sold to others; stims are
taken yourself).

## THE BLACK MARKET DEALER: CAPABILITY KEYSTONE

He sells the machinery of every heist and takes no risk at any of them.

- **Cash** (equipment, dies with you): advanced weaponry · advanced armor
  (125 armor) [requires T1 MaxArmor raise (currently 100) or per-item
  override — implementation dependency, resolve at Black Market
  Dealer build] · gadgets
  — pickpocket kit, handcuffs, unarrest baton.
- **BTC** (entities and unlocks, persist): **the Advanced Hacker Terminal
  unlock** — the only key to the Bank Crypto Vault · machine **jailbreaks**
  (skip a tier at a small risk of bricking the rig or flagging the FBI) ·
  the Chemist's BTC-locked purity tiers are gated the same way.
- **Either**: the Chemist's process accelerators (labor — cash) sit beside
  purity (mastery — BTC) in the same shop, and the split is Law B, not policy.
- **Stimulants** (the Stim War — see THE HOSPITAL): cheap, effective,
  carrying health risks only Medic items cure. He creates the ailment; the
  Hospital sells the cure. His second chemical economy — taken yourself,
  where the Chemist's product is sold to others.

**Because his goods are machines and unlocks, they cost BTC.** His two best
customers — Hacker and Chemist — do not mine by default. So they mine, steal,
or trade. **The Black Market's price list is the demand curve for BTC.**

And: **the Black Market Dealer holds the key to the largest untaxed pile of
money on the server.** Whether he sells the Advanced Terminal unlock, to whom,
and at what price is a live political question. Somebody will try to bribe him
not to. Let them.

*The ladder is safe; the shortcut is not.* That is his whole identity.

## THE HACKER'S DILEMMA

Nothing bars a Hacker from mining. Racks are sold to anyone.

But **installations are exposed**, and a Hacker with a farm is a Hacker with a
base worth raiding. He cannot hack while watching his own buffers. He now
understands, personally, exactly what he has been doing to other people.

Every Hacker chooses:

- **Build** — mine his own BTC, own installations, become prey. A predator
  with roots.
- **Steal** — skim buffers, crack wallets, stay mobile, own nothing that can
  be taken. Slow, risky, pure, broke.
- **Sell** — freelance hacks for other players, paid in coin. Everyone's
  friend, nobody's ally.

**The predator who builds becomes prey.** This is not a rule anyone wrote — it
is Law 1 of the economy doctrine (*capital income is attackable*) applied to
the one job that was exempt from it. He is exempt exactly as long as he owns
nothing.

**The most dangerous man on the server is the one with nothing to lose, and he
can never afford the Advanced Terminal. The one who can afford it has a base.**
Capability and vulnerability are coupled. That coupling is what stops any
single Hacker from becoming unstoppable.

The same shape governs every job: **mastery costs exposure.** Same lesson,
different door.

## ROLE-VACANCY, EXTENDED

Per `ECONOMY_DOCTRINE`: state persists, *interaction* gates on the role.

- No Banker -> the pile holds, pays no yield, and is still robbable.
  Vulnerability while undefended is a feature.
- No Mayor -> the Data Center converts at the **default rate**; City Funds
  still fills; nothing is looted.
- Elected offices vacate per the vote system; an office held by nobody is an
  office nobody is defending.
